Chapter 01/04—The Crown
Brioche Bun

Butter, folded eight times, then left alone.

The dough goes cold before it goes anywhere. Eight folds over three days, each one laminating a little more butter into a crumb that has no business being this light — and then seventy-two hours of nothing at all, which is the part most kitchens skip.

It bakes dark. Not burnt: dark. An egg wash taken to the edge of the amber it is named for, so the crown carries a little bitterness against everything sweet underneath it.

Chapter 02/04—The Melt
Aged Cheddar

Cheddar that waited two years to run.

Twenty-four months in a cellar in the coast range, turned by hand, until the curd has gone crystalline and tastes faintly of stone. A young cheese melts obediently. This one has to be persuaded — and that is the whole point.

It goes on the patty still hot off the iron, under a cloche, for exactly the forty seconds it takes to slump rather than pool. Any longer and two years of work turns into oil.

Chapter 03/04—The Dry Age
Smoked Brisket Patty

Brisket, forty-five days in the cold.

Whole packers hang in a still, dry room at two degrees until they lose a third of their water and gain something else entirely — a smell closer to hazelnut and old paper than to raw beef. The bark that forms is trimmed away and mourned.

Ground once, coarse, and never packed. It meets a plancha at 260°C for ninety seconds a side, which is long enough to build a crust and short enough that the centre never learns what happened.

Chapter 04/04—The Garden
Crisp Greens

Something green, cut four minutes ago.

There is a tray of little gems and mustard frill growing under lamps at the back of the room, and the only thing that qualifies it for this burger is proximity. Four minutes from cut to plate. Nothing travels; nothing wilts on the way.

Dressed in nothing but its own cold. Against forty-five days of beef and two years of cheese, the job of a leaf is to interrupt — one clean, wet, uncomplicated sentence in the middle of a very long paragraph.
